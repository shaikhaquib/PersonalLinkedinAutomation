### Post

Most CRUD apps do not need a UseCase class for every single button tap.

We are writing three classes, four interfaces, and a fake factory just to fetch a user profile.
That is not architecture. That is employment insurance for enterprise developers.
If your domain logic is just calling a repository function and mapping a nullable string, stop.
Just call the repository from the ViewModel.

Use domain layers when business rules are actually complex and tested in pure Kotlin.
For simple data plumbing, the ceremony is just a tax on your shipping speed.

Where do you draw the line between actual domain logic and mindless boilerplate?

### Post

Most Android teams still believe production crashes are always an unhandled exception in their Kotlin code.

Then Android 15 drops, and your crash-free rate tanks while Firebase shows zero stack traces. No uncaught exception handler is fired. The process just vanishes right at the splash screen.

The culprit is native library alignment. When your pre-compiled dependencies use 4KB page sizes instead of the new 16KB standard, the OS kernel loader rejects them at runtime and kills the process silently. 

How are you auditing your third-party SDKs for page size compliance before your next release?

### Post

Tapping pay during peak traffic and the entire checkout screen just froze.

Instantly, the war room started pointing fingers at backend latency and flaky carrier networks. 

The real cause was a heavy main thread Room query triggered directly inside the tap listener. 

Next time, I would enforce strict StrictMode disk read violations in CI from day one so database hits on the UI thread never reach production. 

How often do you find UI freezes actually hiding local data bottlenecks instead of network drops?
